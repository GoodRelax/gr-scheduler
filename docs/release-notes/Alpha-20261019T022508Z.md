This is the first public release of GoodRelax Scheduler (GRS): a schedule chart editor in one HTML file that runs in your browser, with no install and no server.

## What you can do

- Draw a schedule of tasks, milestones and dependencies, and edit it by dragging on the chart or in the property panel.
- Put several bars on one row with task groups, and fold the outline to see as much or as little as you need.
- Track progress with progress markers, and add comment boxes and highlight boxes to the chart.
- Find tasks in the Search Panel and the Resource List, and show only the rows you check.
- Run the Delay Diagnostics: it marks where the schedule slips, explains why, proposes fixes, and saves the fix log as `.tsv`.
- Undo and redo every edit.
- Switch between English and Japanese, and between light and dark.
- Save as GRS JSON; import and export MSPDI (Microsoft Project XML); export the chart as PNG, SVG or a self-contained HTML file.

## What's new for AI users

- **GRS Relay** (`grs-relay.mcpb`): an MCP server for AI desktop apps that support MCP. It passes the AI's requests to the GRS page you have open; the schedule stays in your browser and the relay keeps no copy.
- The AI can read the schedule in small pieces (the Search Panel, the Delay Diagnostics Report, the Resource List), read the schema part by part, and write with commands that are applied all at once or refused as one.
- The AI can run the Delay Diagnostics on the screen, apply its fix proposals, and read or export the fix log, as a person does.
- The AI can export files into a save folder you choose, and ask you to open a file.
- The page reconnects to the relay by itself when the connection drops.

## Known limits

- Drawing is slower than the 60 fps target, most of all on large schedules.
- This is an alpha: file formats and screens may change between versions. Keep a backup of your files.
- Screen readers are not supported.
- The open items are listed in [the handoff](https://github.com/GoodRelax/gr-scheduler/blob/main/docs/development-records/handoff.md) and the [change requests](https://github.com/GoodRelax/gr-scheduler/tree/main/change-request).

## Where your data stays

- GRS keeps your schedule on your own PC. You save it as a file on that PC, and GRS itself sends it to no server.
- GRS Relay talks only to `127.0.0.1`, on the same PC.
- The one exception: whatever you let an AI read through GRS Relay is handled by that AI app, under that app's own terms.
- The server-sync demo talks only to its dummy server on the same PC.

## How to install

- **No download:** open <https://goodrelax.github.io/gr-scheduler/> in your browser.
- **Offline:** download `GRS.html` from <https://goodrelax.github.io/gr-scheduler/download/> (or from the assets of this release), save it anywhere and open it in your browser.
- **With an AI:** download `grs-relay.mcpb` and follow the steps on the download page.
- **Server-sync demo (Windows):** download `grs-server-sync-demo.zip`, unzip it and follow its README.
